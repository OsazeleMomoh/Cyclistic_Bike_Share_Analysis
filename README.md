# Task 4: Cyclistic Bike-Share Analysis (Excel)

This repository will house my work for the Google Data Analysis Capstone Project.
This task entailed the analysis of bike-share data for a company to provide their marketing team with insights into how their annual and casual members use the program differently. This would be sent to my colleagues in the team to give out recommendations, which would then be sent to the director of marketing. I used *Excel* for this task as this would be my first *Excel* dashboard project and the datasets involved were from January to November 2025. This project can be also be done using *R*, *Python* and *SQL*.

- Dataset Link: https://divvy-tripdata.s3.amazonaws.com/index.html

## Dashboard Insights

## Highlights from Analysis
- Cyclistic members use the program more than casual riders.
- Casual riders log in more ride length compared to cyclistic members. 
- There seemed to be a maximum ride length time of almost 1 day in months April and September.
- Electric bikes are preferred more compared to other bike types, by both members and casual riders.
- Users ride more during the weekdays than on the weekends.

## Recommendations for Director
- Target customers from a price-point angle with special offerings and/or perks (like prioritised access to electric bikes), to incentivise casual riders into joining the program.
- Develop the program into a tier-based subscription model to subsidise rides for customers. i.e: members on higher tier pay lower fees for their rides compared to lower tier members and casual users.
- Prioritise members on high-traffic days e.g: members get updates and can reserve their preferred bike types days before access to casual riders
- Introduce a cap on the time that casual members have access to the bikes. When that time is elapsed, they have to pay to continue usage of the bikes. Members can have uncapped and unrestricted access to their bikes.

Nothing beats quality and customer satisfaction. These two factors contribute almost entirely to customer retention even in instances of price increments.
